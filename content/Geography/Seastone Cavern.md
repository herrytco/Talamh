Seastone Cavern is a small, rocky island off the eastern coast of [[High Soria]], a half-day's sail from the nearest fishing hamlets. For nearly the entire year its peak sits just beneath the waves, with the sea over it calm and unremarkable enough that ships crossing the strait rarely suspect anything lies below. For a handful of days each year, an unusually strong seasonal current drains the strait low enough to lift the island clear of the water entirely, an event locals call **the Emergence**. It takes its name from the cave system honeycombing the exposed rock, which despite the crowds the Emergence draws, no one has yet finished mapping.
## The Isle
What breaches the surface during an Emergence is a low, weathered dome of banded grey-blue rock the locals call "seastone," worn glassy-smooth by its months underwater. A single wide mouth opens into the rock near the former waterline, leading into a wide, low antechamber that stays waterlogged and silted even once the tide has fully receded, giving way further back to taller, drier halls the sea can no longer reach.

The deepest hall reached by any recorded visit, a wide chamber the local fishermen call **the Low Door**, ends in a shaft descending further into the rock than the short window of dry access has ever allowed anyone to follow.
## The Emergence
The strait's water level drops for a handful of days once a year, following a seasonal current shift the old almanacs call the Hollow Ebb. The exact timing varies from year to year by as much as a few weeks, and only becomes reliably predictable a day or two in advance, once the water has visibly begun to recede over the island's peak. When the current turns, it turns quickly: the island can vanish beneath the waves again within hours, often with little warning beyond a change in the wind.

Even during the Emergence, the island does not surface cleanly. A ring of seastone spurs surrounds it just beneath the waterline, worn by the current into edges sharp enough to open a hull like a blade rather than simply holing it. Experienced captains creep in along the one or two channels known to be clear, marked from memory or old, jealously guarded charts, while every year brings at least one boat that misjudges the approach and does not sail home. Leaving is worse than arriving: as the current turns and the water rises back over the spurs, they vanish from sight well before they stop being a threat, and more hulls are lost to a hurried departure than to the trip in.
## Scientific and Public Interest
The Emergence has become a modest yearly occasion. Natural philosophers, including standing expeditions sent by the [[High Sorian University]], arrive to study the island's geology and the peculiar regularity of the phenomenon, while merchants and curious travelers turn the nearest fishing hamlet into a brief, crowded festival of chartered boats and overpriced lodging.

For all this attention, no expedition has ever publicly reported reaching past the Low Door. The window is short, the antechamber slow to drain and treacherous underfoot, and every year's Emergence ends the same way: with the tide returning before anyone has gone as far as they'd hoped. What, if anything, any expedition has quietly found beyond that point has never made it into a published account.
## Local Stories
The fishing families who watch the island rise and sink again every year keep a handful of stories about it alive, more from habit than belief, though few of them stay past dark even during the dry days.

- Sailors crossing the strait in the off season sometimes report a low, rhythmic tolling carried up through the water, like a bell struck somewhere below the waves. No bell has ever been recovered, nor has anyone confirmed a source.
- An older generation still speaks of a University expedition, decades back, that pressed on past the Low Door as the water was already returning. The story ends the same way every time it is told: with the party never coming back out, and the university never confirming the trip took place at all.
